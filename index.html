This category is about how people see the dangers around them.
It covers the threats that feel most immediate
and how a sense of safety can shift over time.
It touches on the disasters that disrupt communities
and the larger changes that may shape the years ahead.

The opening chart is based on "What is the single greatest risk to your safety?"
People could choose from 21 answers, grouped into families:

Crime & conflict: crime/violence, war/terrorism

Health: illness, drugs/alcohol/smoking, COVID-19, mental stress

Transportation: road accidents (car, motorbike, truck etc.), other transport accidents (subway, train, plane, etc.)

Money & society: personal finances, the economy, politics/corruption, technology

Daily life: unsafe water, unsafe food, hunger, household accidents, work accidents, drowning

Environment: pollution, climate/severe weather, natural disasters (earthquakes, volcanoes, etc)

Only the top 5 answers are shown for every panel

This category sets what people fear against what they have lived through.
It covers six parts of daily life where serious harm can come from:
unsafe food or water they, violent crime, severe weather, mental health
and dangerous work. For each one, people were asked two things:
how worried they are that it could seriously harm them, and whether they,
or someone they know have actually experienced that harm in the past two years.

For every area, worry and experience are placed next to each other so they can be compared directly.
The difference between them is the point. It shows how closely people's concerns
follow what has actually happened to them.

For many people around the world, a major source of fear is the discrimination they face

They may face discrimination due to the color of their skin,
their gender, their religion, their ethnicity, or a disability

By measuring resilience in four dimensions – individual, household, community and societal,
the Index provides a holistic assessment of resilience available at a global level.
The 2026 Resilience Index is built using blended data from the World Risk Poll,
collected in 2025. It updates the previous Index published in 2022 and 2024,
using the same methodology and with data collected in 2021 and 2023 respectively.

Individual Level:
If a disaster were to occur near you in the future, do you think there is anything
you could do to protect yourself or your family from its impact?

Household Level:
Suppose your household suddenly lost all income and had to survive only
on savings and things that could be sold. How long would your household be able
to cover all the basic needs, such as food, housing, and transportation?

Community Level:
How much do you think most of your neighbours care about you and your wellbeing?
Do you feel safe walking alone at night in the city or area where you live?
Have you helped a stranger or someone you didn’t know who needed help.

Societal Level:
How much do you think the government of cares about you and your wellbeing?
Do you have confidence in each of the following, or not?
-The military?
-The judicial system or courts?
-The national government?
